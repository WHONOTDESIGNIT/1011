**Short answer:** Sarah came to Joli Spa for a course of treatments, went home with a device for the areas she would not show anyone, and booked her next course anyway. That evening at the counter is the product a home line has to be built for, and every difference between a clinic machine and a consumer device follows from it.

## Sarah's evening at Joli Spa

Sarah had booked six sessions on her legs. The course was working, the appointments were in her calendar, and the part she had not planned for was the drive: forty minutes each way, twice a month, plus the wait in a room with a magazine from the previous summer.

On the fourth visit she overheard the receptionist talking to another patient about a device the spa keeps on the counter, for the weeks between appointments. Sarah asked what it was. The receptionist said it was the same kind of light the room uses, in a smaller handset, and that most people take it home for the areas they would rather not have someone else work on.

That was the sentence that decided it. Sarah had never booked a bikini line appointment. She had thought about it, twice, and both times she imagined the room, the paper sheet, the knock at the door.

She bought the device that evening. She also booked her next course.

## What Sarah bought that evening

Three things changed hands at that counter, and only one of them was hardware.

The first was a maintenance task. Between courses, hair grows back more slowly than it did before, and a device at home keeps the legs at the stage the course reached.

The second was privacy. Sarah did the upper lip, the chin and the bikini line herself, in her own bathroom, on a Sunday, with the door locked. No appointment, no paper sheet, nobody counting minutes.

The third was a way to stop paying in advance to a business that might close. Sarah had been through that once at a different place: a course paid up front, a shutters-down notice on the door, and a membership fee she never saw again. A device she owns cannot go out of business.

None of that replaces the six sessions. The legs still need the room, the operator and the window size. What the device did was cover the parts of her life the appointment could not reach.

## Why the counter works

A clinic is the one place where a home device arrives with context. The patient has already felt the light, knows what a session costs, and trusts the person recommending it. That is a better starting position than any marketplace listing, and it is why an equipment brand with clinic accounts can move faster here than a consumer brand starting from nothing.

The clinic gains something too. Sarah's course continued, because the device answered the questions that had been quietly pushing her toward stopping: the drive, the exposure, the prepayment.

## What transfers from a machine business

Two things, and both are expensive to build from scratch.

You know which of your accounts would put a device on the counter and which would let it sit in a cupboard. And you have someone on staff who can read a test report and find the number that does not hold. A new brand has neither.

## What has to be rebuilt

| Your clinic habit | Sarah's reality |
|---|---|
| A trained operator runs the session | She reads the box in a bathroom |
| Someone supervises the treatment | Nobody is in the room |
| Complexity gets absorbed by the operator | Complexity comes back as a return |
| One price, one service contract | A price that has to leave room for the spa's margin |
| A professional file | A consumer file, consumer labelling, a manual for a beginner |

Read that table as a build list.

## The packaging explains before the manual does

A clinic machine arrives in a crate and gets installed by someone who does this for a living. Sarah met her device on a shelf, next to a price card, and decided in the time it took the receptionist to finish a sentence.

That means a structural brief, artwork, a warnings block per market, and a dieline the printer can run without a phone call. Equipment brands underestimate this step because in their world packaging carries logistics. In Sarah's world it carries the sale.

## The price ceiling is set in the marketplace

Your machine is priced against what a clinic earns from using it. Sarah's device is priced against what she already knows: a course of salon sessions, a razor subscription, a competitor's box on a marketplace with forty reviews.

That comparison fixes a ceiling. Your unit cost has to fit under it with room for the spa's margin and a return rate, and the tooling arithmetic follows, because a consumer line sells in thousands and a machine line sells in dozens.

## The certification file starts over

The electrical and optical test reports may overlap with the ones you already hold. The document that travels with the product does not: consumer labelling, a different intended-operator statement, a manual written for someone with no training.

Budget it as a project with a date, the way you would budget a new housing.

## What the board will ask

Four questions, and the answers are short.

**What does the capital buy?** A tooling set, a packaging programme, a consumer file and a returns desk. Those are four purchases, and only the first one looks like manufacturing.

**Will it cannibalise the machines?** Sarah bought a device for areas she would never have booked. When a clinic customer buys a device to avoid buying a machine, the line is mispriced, and that shows up in your first quarter of orders.

**Who handles returns?** Consumer returns arrive at a consumer desk. If your service team has never run one, that is a capability the capital is buying, and it belongs in the budget.

**Which market first?** The one where your accounts already stock consumer products. A clinic that sells skincare to patients can sell a device. A clinic that only runs treatments cannot.

## What a first order looks like

A small run of one platform with your logo, one manual language, and the packaging your first market requires. That is enough to answer two questions: will your accounts stock it, and does your service desk survive the returns.

Ask the factory what the smallest order is for an appearance change. The answer is usually lower than a new brand expects, and it is the cheapest way to find out whether the line is real.

If the pilot sells through and the returns stay quiet, the line has a case. If the devices sit in the cupboard, you learned that for the price of one run, and the machines you already sell lost nothing.
