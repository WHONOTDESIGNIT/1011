**Short answer:** the tenfold gap is window area, energy per pulse, cooling, duty cycle and regulatory class. A clinic machine treats a back in one sitting; a home device treats an area in twenty minutes, once a week. Same physics, different job.

## Five differences that carry the price

| Difference | Clinic machine | Home device |
|---|---|---|
| Window | Several square centimetres, often interchangeable | Under two square centimetres, fixed |
| Energy per pulse | Higher, with operator control | Fixed or gently stepped |
| Cooling | Active, continuous | Contact cooling, intermittent |
| Duty cycle | Runs all day | Minutes of use, then a rest |
| Regulatory class | Professional use, separate pathway | Consumer device, consumer labelling |

A buyer who reads only the joules figure will conclude the home device is a bargain and the clinic machine is a rip-off. Both conclusions are wrong.

## Why the window matters more than the joules

Energy density is joules over area. Enlarge the window and the same joules spread thinner. So a clinic machine can post a similar or lower density than a home device and still deliver more total energy per pulse, over more skin, in less time.

That is why "mine is 6 J/cm²" settles nothing. The question is 6 J/cm² over what area, at what pulse duration, with what cooling underneath.

## The duty cycle is the clinic's real requirement

A salon does not buy one treatment. It buys a machine that works at four in the afternoon after six hours of treatments, with the same output.

Home devices are built for a session. A shift is a different requirement, and the hardware follows it. That single difference explains a large part of the cooling hardware, the power supply and the housing.

## The regulatory class is not paperwork

A professional device and a consumer device are sold under different rules in every market we ship to. The labelling, the claims, the language and the intended operator all change.

Buying a consumer device to use professionally, or a professional machine to resell as consumer equipment, is where the certification stops matching the sale. That mismatch is more expensive than the price gap you were trying to avoid.

## What this means if you are choosing

If you run a treatment room, the decision is about throughput and operator control. If you are building a brand, the decision is about what a buyer can safely do at home without supervision.

Those are different products. A single manufacturer rarely makes both well, and a single price list rarely tells you which one you are reading.

## What a course of clinic treatment asks of a patient

The price is the visible part. A course also asks for a car and the hour either side of the appointment, because the clinic is somewhere you drive to and wait in. It asks a patient to undress an area in a room where someone may knock, and for the bikini line, the underarm, the upper lip or the chin that exposure is often the reason a second session never gets booked, whatever the result of the first.

The money carries a second risk. A course is paid in advance to a business that can stop trading, and when a clinic closes the remaining sessions and the membership fee usually close with it. Patients who have been through that once ask about it before they ask about joules.

None of this makes the clinic a worse place to be treated. It means the clinic sells a finished result on areas that need an operator, and the four frictions above are what keep people away from the rooms that deliver it.

## The device as something the clinic gives away

This is the opening a home device fits into without competing with the treatment room. A patient who finishes a course on the areas that need a trained operator can leave with a device for the areas she would rather not expose to anyone, and for the small areas that are quick to treat alone: the upper lip, the chin, the underarm, the bikini line.

Handed over at the end of a course, the device reads as a gift and a reason to come back for the next course. It also gives the clinic an answer when a patient raises the drive, the exposure or the deposit, because the answer now includes something she takes home.

Two limits belong in the same conversation. The device is not a substitute for the sessions already booked, and it is not for an area that a clinician has told her to leave alone, which is what [the contraindications list](/blog/33-who-should-not-use-ipl-hair-removal-contraindications) covers in full. Say both before the box changes hands, and the gift still lands. What she does the first evening is on [the aftercare page](/blog/ipl-aftercare).

## What you will have to justify internally

If you are the person proposing a home platform for a clinic's retail shelf, you will be asked why it is not simply a cheaper machine.

Bring the five differences as a table. A paragraph loses the comparison. Window, energy per pulse, cooling, duty cycle, regulatory class. Then say which of the five the clinic's treatments depend on. Usually it is the first four, and that is the answer: the home device cannot do the session, and the session is what the clinic sells.

Bring the margin comparison too, honestly. Retail margin per unit is thin. The case for the shelf rests on patient retention between courses. The unit margin alone will not carry it.

## The number that ends the argument

Count patients who completed a course and then stopped booking. Some of them stopped because maintenance at home was easier than another appointment. That is the revenue a maintenance device recovers, and it is measurable from your own booking data in an afternoon.

If that number is small, the shelf is not worth the floor space. Say so. A proposal that survives on a number you can check is worth more than one that survives on enthusiasm.

## What to put in the first order

Order one unit and run it against your own protocol: how long it takes to treat a lower leg, how warm the handset gets, whether the window covers the area you would cover in a session.

Log those three numbers. They are the evidence for whether a home device belongs on your shelf, and they take one afternoon to gather.

Do not order a retail quantity until the trial unit has been used by the people who will sell it. A device nobody has held does not get recommended.
