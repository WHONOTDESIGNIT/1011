IPL device sourcing runs in five stages: spec and quote, supplier verification, tooling and first article, certification, then mass production. Budget eight to nine months end to end. The stages that run long are rarely the ones brands plan for.

I'm Eric, co-founder of iShine. We run ODM and OEM programs for brands including Costco, Cyden and Haier, and we have watched these five stages go wrong in most of the ways they can. What follows is the map. Each stage links to the detail if you want it.

## The five stages of IPL device sourcing

| Stage | What it locks in | What it can still change later |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spec and quote | Form factor, performance envelope, cost band | Almost anything, at a price |
| Verification | Whether the supplier is who they say | Nothing, if you discover this late |
| Tooling and first article | Housing geometry, fit and finish | Everything downstream |
| Certification | Which markets you can legally sell into | Nothing cheaply |
| Mass production | Delivery dates, working capital | The launch calendar |

Read that middle column again. Verification is the only stage that locks in something you cannot buy your way out of later.

## Stage 1: The spec and the quote

Two things come out of this stage: a document that says what the device does, and a number that says what it costs to build. Getting the second without the first is how projects start in the wrong place.

The cost bands are wide and worth knowing before any conversation. ODM development including tooling lands between $30,000 and $100,000. OEM development starts at $80,000 and climbs past $250,000, with tooling alone at $20,000 to $50,000.

A quote with four lines is not a quote. [What a real quote contains](/blog/ipl-device-manufacturer-price) breaks down the six lines that should be there, and why a $12 unit price quietly becomes $19.

Volume is the other half of this stage. ODM usually starts at 500 units, OEM at 1,000 to 5,000, and the difference in unit price between those tiers is almost entirely fixed cost looking for somewhere to land. [The MOQ arithmetic](/blog/ipl-device-moq-explained) shows the formula and the two legitimate ways to push the number down.

## Stage 2: Verification, before money moves

This is the stage that eliminates candidates. Do it before a deposit, not after.

Start with documents. Seven of them, and one test that matters more than the rest: whether four legal identities match across the paperwork. The business license entity, the applicant on the safety certificate, the FDA establishment registration and the invoice header have to be the same person, or three of them plus a documented affiliate. When they diverge, the manufacturing you are buying is not the manufacturing you are being shown.

[The seven documents to request](/blog/how-to-vet-ipl-device-manufacturer) covers each one and what a fake version looks like. If you only have one afternoon, do this before anything else.

Then look at the building. If you can fly, fly. If you cannot, a single unedited video call on a production day gets most of the value: fixed route, random unit opened on camera, and a look at the reject bin. [The 90-minute remote audit](/blog/remote-factory-audit-ipl-device) has the route and the five things to ask them to show.

What you are checking in both cases is not cleanliness. It is whether the paperwork and the floor describe the same company.

## Stage 3: Tooling and first article

The mold is cut here, and once it exists you have committed. Everything downstream, from fit and finish to how the device feels in the hand, traces back to this decision.

Tooling runs $20,000 to $50,000 for a housing set, depending on part count and finish. A shared mold costs near zero and gives you a product with someone else's silhouette. Neither is wrong; they lead to different products.

First article approval is the gate. One unit comes off the line, gets measured against the drawing, and gets signed off. A factory that wants to skip this step is telling you they expect the first production run to be the experiment.

The components inside the housing matter as much as the housing. The capacitor bank decides whether the device still delivers its rated energy after two hundred shots, and the adapter decides whether it survives a European winter socket. [Which components hold up](/blog/components-that-stand-up-to-scrutiny) names what we specify and why.

If you are still choosing between building your own design and customizing an existing platform, [OEM versus ODM](/blog/02-oem-vs-odm-choosing-the-right-manufacturing-model) compares the money, the timeline and the IP position directly.

## Stage 4: Certification

Nothing ships without this, and nothing else in the project can compress it.

The appliance is assessed against EN IEC 60335-2-23 in our test files, on top of the general IEC 60335-1. Separately, photobiological safety is assessed under [IEC 62471](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/7076). For the US, clearance is checked in the [FDA 510(k) database](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpmn/pmn.cfm); for Europe, [Regulation (EU) 2017/745](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/745/oj) governs the route.

Which certificates you actually need depends on volume. At 1 to 100 units a month you are selling a consumer appliance. Between 100 and 3,000 the quality system becomes the thing that gets audited. Past 3,000 to 5,000 a month, a 510(k) or MDR posture usually comes into play. [The compliance ladder](/blog/ipl-validation-services) maps that progression, including the difference between consumer-electronics CE and MDR CE.

The window itself is roughly 124 days on a 253-day project. That number is why the launch calendar gets decided in this stage rather than in the next one.

## Stage 5: Mass production and the shipping nobody budgets

Production is the stage where plans made in January get tested in September.

The two things that surprise brands here are packaging and seasonality. A gift box that does not fit the shipper means you buy a mailer and a master carton, and both have minimum print quantities. [Shipping and packaging structure](/blog/ipl-device-shipping-packaging-gift-box-remailer-master-carton) covers the three layers and what each one costs to change.

Seasonality decides when the factory floor is available. Home beauty devices peak into Black Friday and again before summer, and both peaks consume tooling slots and line time well before the shopping begins. [The order calendar](/blog/ipl-order-calendar-black-friday-summer-peak) works backwards from a sale date to the latest safe start.

## Where the 253 days actually go

A full-process development cycle runs 253 days across 14 stages. The distribution is not intuitive: certification takes about 124 of those days, and tooling, which is not on the stage list at all, is what makes the dates slip when it runs late.

[The stage-by-stage timeline](/blog/ipl-hair-removal-device-development-timeline) has the day counts. Read it before you commit to a launch date, not after.

## The decision that costs the most to get wrong

Most of the value sits in Stage 2. Everything after it is engineering and project management, both of which are recoverable with money and attention. A supplier that fails verification is not recoverable, because you find out after the deposit, after the tooling, and often after the packaging is printed.

If you are already in a relationship that is drifting, [switching manufacturers](/blog/switching-ipl-manufacturers) walks through the four costs of a move, and the mold is the cheapest of them.

## Where to start with IPL device sourcing

Write the spec. Ask two factories for a quote with six lines and a validity window. Ask both for the seven documents. Compare the paperwork before you compare the price.
