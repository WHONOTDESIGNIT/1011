**Short answer:** the hardware knowledge transfers. Power electronics, thermal design and housing carry over. The claim language does not. UV-C sells a dose against a surface; IPL sells an energy density against skin. A germ claim on an IPL device is unsupported in every market we ship to.

## The same word, two different products

"Light-based device" covers a UV-C steriliser, an LED nail lamp, a red light panel and an IPL handset. They share a power supply.

| | UV-C | IPL |
|---|---|---|
| Target | Microbes on a surface or in air | Hair follicle under skin |
| Claim basis | Log reduction at a defined dose | Hair reduction over a treatment course |
| Safety frame | Eye and skin exposure limits | Photobiological safety of the lamp |
| Typical regulation | Biocide or electrical | Electrical device, market-specific |

A factory that has built UV-C products has real advantages in power electronics, thermal design and housing. None of them is a germ claim.

## Where the crossover actually pays

- **Optical design.** Filters, reflectors and window materials are shared problems.
- **Thermal management.** Both products run a light source hard and need to shed heat.
- **Manufacturing discipline.** Ingress protection, drop tolerance, and label durability.
- **Supplier base.** Lamp, quartz and driver suppliers overlap.

That is a genuine head start on hardware.

## The three claims to delete

Three phrases turn up in IPL copy written by hygiene companies:

1. Any germ, bacteria or sterilising claim.
2. Any "safe for all skin" or "no side effects" line.
3. Any medical claim, including "treats" or "cures".

Replace them with what the test report supports: the energy density, the window area, the pulse duration, the cooling, and the contraindications.

## What a buyer will ask you

A distributor will ask for the file before the wavelength: the test reports for the device, the declaration that it meets the rules of the destination market, and the market list showing which of those documents exists for each country you plan to sell into and how early in the order it has to be ready.

Start with that.

## What we do, and what we do not

We build IPL devices and we hand over the test reports and declarations per market. We do not sell disinfecting products, we do not advise on biocide claims, and we do not write claims we cannot support with a report. Deliverable documentation differs by market, and we confirm the list per market before quoting an order date.

## What you will have to justify to your own product team

Your product team will argue about the light source first. Bring two lists into that meeting: the claims you cannot make, and the evidence behind each refusal. A germ claim needs a biocide route. A "safe for all skin" line needs a population that does not exist. A medical claim needs a device classification you do not have.

Then bring what you can say: energy density, window area, pulse duration, cooling, contraindications. Every one of those comes from a test report, so every one of them survives a distributor's question.

## The cost of getting this wrong

An overreaching claim fails at retail, where the buyer's compliance team reads the pack. The whole range comes off the shelf while the copy is rewritten.

Put that in front of your product team: a season's revenue against the cost of a relabel.

## The first order, if you get that far

Buy one unit and read its packaging against your own compliance thinking. What you are measuring is how much of the file you would have to rebuild to sell it confidently.

Then compare the manual language with the market list. If the device ships in one language and you sell in four, that gap is your first project, and it sits on your side.
