**Short answer:** a home IPL device sold in Japan needs the PSE mark under the Electrical Appliances and Materials Safety Act. Japanese practice has not settled which of the two categories home IPL belongs to. Most devices are handled as non-specified appliances with the circle mark, a minority as specified appliances with the diamond mark. The factory supplies the file. The Japanese importer files the notification and carries the legal responsibility.

## Why Japan is not a "send a sample and see" market

Most markets let a brand test demand before the compliance spend. Japan rarely works that way. The mark goes on the product and the packaging before the first commercial unit moves, and the notification is tied to a legal entity that has to exist in Japan.

So the sequence runs backwards from what a new brand expects. You find the importer first. The importer tells you how they intend to classify the device. The classification determines the test basis. The test basis determines the label. Only then do you print packaging.

Get that order wrong and the artwork is reprinted.

## The classification problem, stated plainly

The law splits electrical appliances into two groups. Specified appliances carry the diamond mark and need conformity assessment by a registered body. Non-specified appliances carry the circle mark and allow self-declaration backed by test reports.

For mature product categories the split is a lookup. For home IPL it isn't. The category is recent enough that laboratories, importers and consultants read it differently, and no single authority has published a line that everyone works from. In practice the market has settled into a rough pattern: most home IPL devices are handled as non-specified appliances, a minority are treated as specified.

That is not a loophole. It is what happens when a product category arrives faster than the guidance does.

| | Circle PSE (non-specified) | Diamond PSE (specified) |
|---|---|---|
| Who assesses | The business operator, supported by test reports | A registered conformity assessment body |
| Evidence held | Test reports and an attestation of test results | A certificate from the assessment body |
| What the factory supplies | Technical file, test reports, label artwork | Technical file, samples, test reports |
| Practical share for home IPL | The common route | The minority route |
| Can a factory outside Japan hold the notification | No | No |
| Effect on launch timeline | Gated by the importer's internal review | Gated by the assessment body's queue |

Whichever route your importer takes, put it in writing before the packaging order goes out. If they cannot state which one they intend to use and why, the project is not ready to print. A brand that discovers the route after printing pays for the plates twice.

## Who files, and in whose name

The notification to the regional bureau of METI is filed by the business operator placing the product on the Japanese market. For an imported device that is the importer, not the factory.

Three consequences follow, and they surprise people:

- The importer's name and address go on the label, not the factory's, and not the brand's unless the brand itself is the Japanese entity.
- The importer becomes the party the authorities contact. Every question about the product reaches them first.
- The importer has to be able to produce inspection records, and their record keeping depends on what the factory sends and whether it arrives in a form they can file.

A brand with no Japanese entity is therefore not the responsible party, even if it owns the trademark. That is the structural reason Japanese importers hold the upper hand in the first year.

## What the importer will ask the factory for

The same six documents come up every time. Assemble them once as a pack rather than per project.

| Document | Detail the importer needs | Common gap |
|---|---|---|
| Test reports | Which standards, which laboratory, which model, issue dates | Report covers a different variant than the one being imported |
| Technical file | Construction, ratings, component list, circuit information | Component list names parts by trade name only, without ratings |
| Rating and label artwork | Voltage, frequency, power, mark placement | Artwork prepared before the importer's identity was known |
| Inspection records | Routine inspection items and results, in a retainable format | Records kept in a form the importer cannot file |
| Traceability | Batch or serial coding, and where it appears | No code at all, so a recall cannot be scoped |
| Attestation and mandate | Who declares what, and on whose behalf | The attestation is signed by a party that is not the notifying business |

That last row causes the most rework. When the notification sits with the importer, an attestation signed by the factory does not line up with the file the importer has to keep. The fix is a one-page document map agreed at the start: which document, issued by whom, held by whom.

## What the label has to carry

```
PSE mark:          <circle or diamond, per the route agreed in writing>
Business operator: <importer name>, <address>, <phone>
Manufacturer:      <factory legal entity, country>
Model:             <model code used in the test reports>
Rating:            <V, Hz, W>
```

The model code line matters more than it looks. Japanese importers cross-check the label against the test report, and a mismatch between the code on the box and the code on the report is a rejection at their internal review, not at customs. Agree one code, use it on every document.

## What this means for the timeline

Compliance work in Japan is sequential, and it starts after the importer relationship exists.

1. Importer identified, route agreed in writing.
2. Test reports obtained or confirmed against the applicable standards.
3. Notification filed by the importer.
4. Label artwork updated with the importer's details, then printed.
5. First production run, with inspection records formatted for the importer's file.

Steps two and four are the ones a factory controls, and they are the two that most often delay a launch. The artwork cannot be finalised before the importer's identity is known, and reports cannot be reused if the device was changed after testing.
