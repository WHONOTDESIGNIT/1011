**Short answer:** the client who comes back every three weeks already trusts your judgment. Put one or two home IPL devices on the shelf, introduce them when she asks, and when she says nothing, let a conversation about hair or skin open the door. The rest of this article is about what your staff has to be able to say once that door is open.

## The client you already have

A regular client books every few weeks, and her hands are occupied for most of the appointment. That is an hour of conversation covering skin, hair and what she has already tried.

After a year or two of that, she stops asking what things cost and starts asking what you recommend. She buys the base coat a technician suggests because the suggestion came from someone whose last five suggestions worked.

A stranger reading reviews has nothing like it.

## What the third year makes possible

The same client mentions the hair on her upper lip, or the laser price she looked up once and stopped at. She asks whether the device on the shelf would work for her.

The device is not the subject of that question. She is asking whether your judgment extends to this, which is the one thing the counter can supply and a marketplace listing cannot.

## One or two devices, and the room stays the room

A nail salon that fills a wall with devices turns into a shop with chairs. The version that works keeps the counter, keeps the appointment, and sets out one or two devices where a client can pick them up.

Two is a reasonable ceiling for a first year: one for the team to use, one to sell. When the second one leaves the shop, reorder it and mention it to the client who bought the first.

## When she asks

The question usually comes in one of three shapes: does this work, does it hurt, and how often. Thirty seconds each, in your own words.

*Does it work?* It slows regrowth over weeks, and it maintains what a course of professional treatment has already done. It treats a small area at a time.

*Does it hurt?* Warm, like a rubber band flicking. It goes down on the lower settings.

*How often?* Once a week on most areas, with a rest if the skin stays pink.

If the technician has to reach for the box to answer those three, the shelf waits. Training for this takes one page and a handset the staff have held themselves.

## When she says nothing

Most clients never ask, so the device stays invisible unless the conversation reaches it. Two topics get there without a pitch.

The first is the hair a client is already fighting: the upper lip, the chin, the neck, the arm. The second is the skin she is unhappy about: ingrown hairs, darker patches from shaving, irritation under the jaw.

Both come up while her hands are occupied and she is talking anyway. Naming the device once, inside that conversation, is the whole technique.

## The two devices in the room are not the same light

Your client already associates you with a device that shines light on a hand. The difference is what the light reaches, and it is worth saying out loud once.

| The gel lamp | The home IPL device |
|---|---|
| The light cures a coating on the nail | The light puts energy into skin |
| Too long wastes a set | Too much irritates skin |
| No contraindications | Medication, skin condition, recent treatments |
| A mistake costs a manicure | A mistake costs a client |

## Three clients to turn away

- Anyone on a photosensitising medication, whatever the dose.
- Anyone with an active skin infection or broken skin on the area.
- Anyone who had a wax or epilation on that area in the last two weeks.

Say it plainly, once, and hand over [the full contraindication list](/blog/33-who-should-not-use-ipl-hair-removal-contraindications). Then let the client think it over.

## What the owner needs before the first sale

Four things to settle before the first device leaves the shelf.

**A one-page script.** The three exclusions, the three answers, and the line that says come back in eight weeks. Written down, it survives a busy Saturday.

**The insurance question.** Check whether your policy covers advice about skin. Find out which one you hold before the first device leaves the shelf.

**The return rate.** Watch it from the first quarter. One return costs more than one nail set's margin, which is the reason a sale without the exclusions is not a sale.

**The first order.** One unit for the staff, two for sale. [The private label terms](/services/private-label#commercial-terms-section) state the minimum order, the price band and the tooling rules; the platforms themselves are on [the products page](/products).

## Where it goes in the room

The pedicure corner, where a conversation about legs already happens and the client's hands are free.

Next to the polish wall, it reads as something to add to the basket, and basket items in a service business are the ones clients come back for.

## What two years of trust is worth

Count the clients who have been with you for more than a year. Each one already takes your word on a base coat, which is the only credential the device needs.

The shelf needs three things: a technician who has held the handset, one page of script, and one or two devices within reach.
