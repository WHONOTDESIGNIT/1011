Related reading: [How to read Manufacturer, Contract Manufacturer and Foreign Exporter in FDA registration and listing](/blog/35-fda-establishment-registration-device-listing-roles-manufacturer-contract-manufacturer-foreign-exporter) and [finding an FDA-cleared manufacturer rather than a trading company](/blog/14-fda-cleared-ipl-device-manufacturer-guide).

If you are a brand owner buying an FDA-cleared IPL and putting your own name on it, the question that decides your regulatory workload is not *whose brand is on the box*. It is *which regulated activities you actually perform*.

On the common private-label route — you source an unchanged, already-cleared device, deliver it to a US importer, and develop neither hardware nor firmware — the manufacturer normally remains the **legal manufacturer, the 510(k) holder, the labeler of record and the foreign exporter**. You do not become the specification developer by owning the brand, and you do not acquire a registration obligation merely by having your name on the product. Getting your brand into GUDID is then a choice between two account routes, and that choice is what moves responsibility.

Both sides of a private-label negotiation tend to guess, and they guess in opposite directions. The brand owner assumes it must register; the factory assumes it is "just the supplier." Both are usually wrong, and the cost of guessing is either an unnecessary registration or an unowned obligation.

## The one rule that settles most private-label arguments

FDA obligations attach to **activities**, not to brand ownership. A brand name on a label is not itself a regulated activity. Registration, listing, UDI submission, complaint handling and MDR reporting attach to whichever party performs the regulated activity that triggers them.

That is why the productive question is not "who owns the brand?" but "which of the roles below does each party perform, and can it evidence it?"

A trademark is a commercial right. It confers no regulatory function. This is why the same device can carry a well-known brand while the manufacturer remains the 510(k) holder, the labeler of record and the foreign exporter.

## The seven roles — and what each one actually triggers

These are the roles that appear in a private-label IPL chain. Only some of them create duties, and only some of them are visible in the public FDA database.

| Role | What it means | Visible in FDA's public database? |
|---|---|---|
| **Legal manufacturer** | Designs and manufactures the finished device; owns the design history file | Yes — as *Manufacturer* |
| **Contract manufacturer** | Produces to another party's specification; may or may not hold the clearance | Yes — as *Contract Manufacturer* |
| **Foreign exporter** | Exports the device from the manufacturing country; a registered establishment operation | Yes — as *Foreign Exporter* |
| **510(k) holder** | Owns the premarket notification the device is marketed under | Not as a label; traceable via the submission number |
| **Labeler of record** | The party whose label is applied, and who answers to FDA for that label | No — inferred from the label |
| **Specification developer** | The party that develops the specifications the device is built to | No |
| **GUDID account holder** | Holds the GUDID account and the device identifier (DI) records for the platform | No — only as the record's Company Name |

**Four of these seven are invisible in public data.** That is why a screenshot of a supplier's FDA listing is weak evidence of who answers to FDA for the label — and why buyers who rely on the listing alone keep asking the same question one email later.

A factory that genuinely carries several of these roles can evidence them rather than assert them: an establishment registration number, an owner/operator number, a DUNS number, the 510(k) number, and the GUDID account. For this platform, our registered positions are establishment registration **3021338880**, owner/operator **10086240**, DUNS **620658743**, cleared under **K222537**, with listing activities recorded as Manufacturer, Foreign Exporter and Contract Manufacturer.

## Adding your brand to GUDID — the two paths

GUDID (the Global Unique Device Identification Database) is where device identifier records live. If your device is a US-marketed Class II IPL, it needs a DI record. In a private-label relationship there are exactly two arrangements, and the difference between them is **who the legal labeler is** — which is also what decides your registration duty.

### Option 1 — a new DI filed under the manufacturer's GUDID account

The manufacturer holds the GUDID account. When your brand is applied, the product is identified under **your brand name**, which means it is identified as **its own device configuration** — so it needs **its own DI and its own GUDID record**, filed by the manufacturer under the manufacturer's account. Because the label is applied by the manufacturer, and the record is filed under the manufacturer's account:

- the **manufacturer is the labeler of record** and the party answerable to FDA;
- the **DI record is filed under the manufacturer's account**;
- **you do not need to register** as an establishment for this activity.

This is the lower-effort route, and usually the right one when your involvement is sourcing, branding and distribution rather than device design.

One point of vocabulary, because it causes real confusion in GUDID discussions: **whether a brand-name change is a data update to an existing DI record or an event that requires a new DI is a fact about the field, not a matter of preference — and it is answered in one specific place.** GUDID business rules for each data attribute are set out in FDA's **GUDID Data Elements Reference Table (DERT)**, which includes a **"New DI Trigger?"** determination per field. Set against that: Brand Name is a **DI-level** attribute (GUDID holds the DI portion of the UDI; production identifiers are not stored, only flagged), and the triggers are generally framed around whether the device is still the same device — things such as sterilisation state or single-use attributes. Whether brand name sits inside or outside that logic is exactly what the DERT column settles. Check it before you commit to either workflow, because the two paths differ: a data update to an existing record is a different operation from assigning a new DI and submitting a new record.

### Option 2 — your own GUDID account

If you or your US entity holds a GUDID account, the manufacturer can act as your **authorised third-party submitter** and file on your behalf. Here the **UDI is yours**, you are the **legal labeler of record**, and the obligations that attach to the labeler attach to you — including the accuracy of the DI record and the post-market duties that follow the label.

Option 2 does not merely move a data record; it moves *responsibility*. Under Option 2, complaint handling, corrections and removals, and MDR reporting obligations follow the labeler. Elect it deliberately, not by accident of who happened to create the account.

| Question | Points to |
|---|---|
| Do you develop or change hardware, firmware or specifications? | **Option 2** — you are moving into specification-developer territory |
| Do you need the DI record in your own name for your own systems? | **Option 2** |
| Is your role sourcing, branding and distributing an unchanged device? | **Option 1** |
| Do you want no FDA registration obligation at all? | **Option 1** |

## What to decide before the GUDID record is created

Two decisions must be made *before* artwork is finalised, because both are expensive to change afterwards.

**1. The brand name is a data element, and it is a string.** GUDID records the brand name as the proprietary or trade name used on the device label or catalogue — a text value, not an image. Product artwork frequently carries a logotype or a device mark, and that artwork belongs on the physical label; it is not what the record holds.

| Carrier | The logo | The brand name |
|---|---|---|
| **GUDID DI record** | Not held — the field is a text value | Brand name as text; accepted characters per the DERT |
| **Physical label and packaging** | The full logo device, in your artwork | Brand name as printed |

- **Character set: verify it in the DERT, because it is the one place that answers it.** GUDID business rules for each data attribute — including the accepted characters for a field — are published in FDA's **GUDID Data Elements Reference Table (DERT)**. This is worth doing rather than assuming, and the reason is a distinction that is easy to get backwards: the strict **ISO/IEC 646** character restriction is associated with the **device identifier (DI) itself**, while **Brand Name** is a descriptive string data element validated by the GUDID business rules. Whether a trademark symbol such as **®** or **™** passes is therefore a question about the Brand Name field's rule in the DERT — check that row before you rely on it, because a rejected character at submission costs a rework cycle.
- **Logo artwork stays on the label, not in the field.** Brand Name is a text value; a logotype or device mark is not what the field holds. The logo still prints on the physical label and packaging — that is your artwork, and it is unaffected by the record.

This is not a workaround; it is the normal division between a data record and label artwork. But it has to reach the designer early, because it changes the artwork brief.

**2. The device model designation.** GUDID records are keyed to a device configuration, not to a marketing campaign. Fix the model designation before the record is created, and settle the two open DERT questions above at the same time — because whether a later identification change means editing an existing record or assigning a new DI depends on that same DERT row, and the cheaper sequence is to know which before you name anything.

> The two questions marked above are deliberately left as DERT checks rather than stated as settled facts: the accepted character set for Brand Name, and whether a brand-name change is flagged as a **New DI Trigger**. Both are answered in the **GUDID Data Elements Reference Table**, which FDA maintains and updates — so the row in the current DERT, not an inference from the general rule, is the reliable answer.

## Does a private-label change need a new 510(k)?

This is the question brand owners ask second, and it has a documented answer. FDA's guidance *Deciding When to Submit a 510(k) for a Change to an Existing Device* provides a logic scheme — and it is explicit that a "documentation" outcome is **not** a free pass.

FDA's main flowchart asks, in order:

1. **Is the change made with the intent to significantly improve the safety or effectiveness of the device?** For example to mitigate a known risk, or in response to adverse events. If yes, a **new 510(k) is likely required**, regardless of the remaining questions.
2. **Is it a labelling change?** → go to **Flowchart A**.
3. **Is it a technology, engineering or performance change?** → **Flowchart B** (or D for IVDs).
4. **Is it a materials change?** → **Flowchart C** (or D for IVDs).

Two terms carry specific meaning in the flowcharts, and misreading them causes most of the confusion: **"new 510(k)"** means a new 510(k) is *likely required*, while **"documentation"** means a new 510(k) is *likely not required — document your analysis and file it for future reference*.

Take the activity set a brand owner typically describes: source an unchanged, cleared device from China, deliver it to a US importer, under the customer's brand, with no hardware or firmware development. The change is **labelling**, so the assessment runs through **Flowchart A**, not the technology or materials charts. The first question still controls: if the labelling change is intended to significantly improve safety or effectiveness, a new 510(k) is likely required regardless. Otherwise the analysis is documented and filed.

The single most consequential misreading is this: **"no new 510(k)" and "no work" are not the same statement.** The quality system regulation applies *whether or not* premarket review is triggered. Review and approval of design and production changes (21 CFR 820.30, 820.70) and documentation in the device master record (21 CFR 820.181) still apply — and those records are reviewable during an FDA inspection under FD&C Act §704(e).

A further nuance from the guidance: for many changes, submission of a new 510(k) is not required precisely because reliance on existing quality-system requirements is considered the least burdensome way to reasonably assure the safety and effectiveness of the changed device.

## The role that must exist in every variant — the US initial importer

Whatever route you choose, one US-side role has to exist: an **initial importer with a US place of business**. This requirement attaches to the *importing activity*, not to brand ownership. In practice it can be your US entity, a US agent, a US distributor, or a compliance-capable import partner.

If you are an EU company with no US establishment, you will need either that importer or a registration path of your own — and which one follows from the Option 1 / Option 2 election above.

## The provisional responsibility matrix

The matrix below is assessed **on activities**, and it holds regardless of the name on the label. It is provisional in only three places, because those three depend on naming and route.

| Role / obligation | Manufacturer | Brand owner (on the sourced-and-supplied activity) |
|---|---|---|
| Legal manufacturer | **Yes** | No |
| Contract manufacturer | **Yes** | No |
| Foreign exporter | **Yes** — already registered | No |
| 510(k) holder | **Yes** | No |
| Labeler of record | **Yes** under Option 1; third-party submitter under Option 2 | **Yes** under Option 2 only |
| Specification developer | **Yes** | Not on this activity — **not automatic**, and not conferred by brand ownership |
| GUDID account / DI records | **Yes** — holds the account; files the new DI record carrying your brand name (Option 1), or files as third-party submitter (Option 2) | Optional — only if you hold your own account |
| UDI / DI submission | Submits as labeler under Option 1; as authorised third party under Option 2 | Submits, or authorises the manufacturer, under Option 2 |
| Initial importer | No | **Must exist in the chain, with a US place of business** |
| US agent | **Yes** — on file | Only if you register as a foreign establishment |
| Establishment registration & device listing | **Yes** | Only if you become labeler of record or specification developer |
| Post-market: MDR reporting, corrections and removals, complaint handling | **Yes**, as manufacturer and labeler of record | Distributor-level record-keeping; full reporting duties arise only on becoming labeler of record |

The only branching decision is Option 1 versus Option 2. Everything else follows from activities, and does not change when the brand name changes.

## What a manufacturer needs from you before it can sign a final determination

A provisional matrix does not depend on naming. A **final, signed** determination does, in exactly three places:

1. **Brand name and model designation** as they will appear on the label.
2. **Label artwork or a mock-up**, showing placement of the brand, the manufacturer and the importer identification — because whether you become a *relabeler* turns on whether **label content changes**, not on whether your name appears.
3. **The route elected — Option 1 or Option 2 — and who the US initial importer will be.**

With those three, the manufacturer's regulatory function can sign a reasoned determination covering whether a new 510(k), a letter-to-file, or no submission is required for the labelling; the DI decision; and the listing owner.

Ask for the provisional matrix before naming is fixed. A supplier that will only answer after the artwork exists has the sequence backwards.

## Closing — the question to ask a prospective manufacturer

Ask which of the seven roles the factory performs, and ask it to evidence each one. A factory that holds and can evidence several — registration, listing, the clearance, the GUDID account — is telling you something about how much of your compliance burden it can carry. A factory that describes itself only as "the supplier" is leaving that burden, and its ownership, undecided.
