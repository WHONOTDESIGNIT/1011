An IPL device manufacturer price from a Chinese factory lands between $30,000 and $100,000 in development and tooling before you have one sellable unit. OEM development starts at $80,000 and climbs past $250,000. The per-unit number on page one is rarely what you end up paying, and the line count of a quote tells you more about the final invoice than the total does.

I'm Eric, and I price these projects at iShine for brands including Costco and Cyden. The numbers below come from quotes we've issued and from competitor quotes brands have asked us to review.

We quote these projects every week. We also get sent competitor quotes by brands who want a second opinion, which is a useful place to sit, because we see the same three or four shapes over and over.

## The number on the first page is never the cost

A quote that reads "IPL device, 3,000 units, $12.80/unit" is not a quote. It's a placeholder.

Here's what actually has to be paid for on a home IPL device: the mold set for the housing, the electronics design if it isn't a shared platform, the safety and photobiological testing, the packaging artwork and its print plates, certification samples that get destroyed in the lab, freight, and whatever the factory is doing with your money between deposit and shipment. That's six or seven distinct costs, and only one of them scales with unit count.

If none of the other six appear on the paper, they didn't disappear. They moved.

## The six lines a complete IPL quote has

This is what a quote looks like when a factory expects you to compare it against another one.

| Line | What it covers | Typical range | Where it's negotiable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tooling | Housing mold set, sometimes a second mold for a variant color | $20,000–$50,000 | Sharing an existing mold cuts this to near zero |
| NRE / development | Electronics, firmware, mechanical design for OEM | $0 for ODM, $20,000+ for OEM | Scope: fewer custom features, less NRE |
| Certification | Safety, EMC, photobiological testing | Priced per standard, per sample | Reusing an existing platform report |
| Packaging | Artwork, print plates, first run | Low thousands, but re-quoted on every design change | Standard carton vs custom gift box |
| Certification samples | Units destroyed by the lab | 5–20 units | Almost never |
| Payment terms | Deposit %, balance trigger | 30/70 is common, 50/50 happens | Milestone structure |

Those certification lines aren't padding. A home IPL device sits under [IEC 60335-2-113](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/67733), the particular requirements for cosmetic appliances built around lasers and intense light sources, and separately under [IEC 62471](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/7076) for photobiological safety. Both are billed per standard and per sample, which is why the line doesn't shrink when your order does. If the product is heading to the US, confirm the clearance in the [FDA 510(k) database](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpmn/pmn.cfm) rather than on a certificate PDF. In Europe the same gate is [Regulation (EU) 2017/745](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/745/oj), which is why the certification line reappears the moment the legal manufacturer changes. ## Why a $12 unit price ends up at $19

The arithmetic of the tail is boring and predictable. Here's how it goes wrong.

You agree $12.80 at 3,000 units. Then the housing needs a second mold because the first one has a visible weld line on the A-surface. That's new tooling, quoted mid-project, when you have no room to push back. Then certification: the lab asks for eight more samples because the first batch had a variance in output. Then packaging, because the gift box you designed doesn't fit the shipper and now you need both a mailer and a master carton. Then freight, because the initial price was FOB and you assumed delivered.

None of these are scams. They're just costs that existed from day one and weren't written down. Our own [development timeline breakdown](/blog/ipl-hair-removal-device-development-timeline) puts the certification window at 124 days of a 253-day schedule. A quarter of the project sits in a lab, and labs bill per sample.

The version of this that *is* a scam is different, and we wrote about it separately in [the ugly truth about IPL manufacturers](/blog/ugly-truth-ipl-manufacturers-energy-adapter-fda). Fake energy output, forged certificates, adapters that fail on the first cold morning. That article is about people who intend to deceive. This one is about people who don't, and still produce a number you can't budget against.

## What to ask before you compare two quotes

Send both factories these four questions. The replies are more informative than the prices.

Ask which mold the tooling line buys, and whether it's new or shared. A shared mold means the $20,000 is not there, and it also means your housing looks like someone else's housing.

Ask what happens to the certification cost if you order 500 units instead of 3,000. If the answer is "it's the same," the certification is being amortized into unit price, and your unit price is lying to you about its own structure.

Ask for the quote in a spreadsheet, not a PDF. A factory that can only produce a PDF is a factory that doesn't want you doing arithmetic.

Ask how long the price holds and what would change it.

## The three questions that reveal a trading company

Plenty of "manufacturers" are trading companies with a nice website. Some of them are fine, and they add real logistics value. But they should not be quoting you as if they own a mold.

Ask for the business license and check whether the registered scope includes manufacturing, not just sales.

Ask which factory physically builds the unit, and whether you can visit it. A real factory says yes and gives you an address. A trading company says the factory doesn't allow visitors.

Ask for a photo of the mold with the tooling number on it. Molds get stamped. Trading companies don't have access to that.

## What we tell brands who ask for a number first

Send us your spec and we'll give you a range before you send us a drawing. Ranges are honest at that stage. A single number at that stage is a guess dressed up as engineering.

The genuine floor for a compliant home IPL device, safety tested, photobiologically evaluated and packaged with a mold that produces a clean housing, is not a $12 device. If someone quotes you under that floor with all six lines included, ask what they left out. There's always something.
