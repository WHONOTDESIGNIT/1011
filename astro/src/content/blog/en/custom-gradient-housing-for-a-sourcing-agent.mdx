## The Call That Could Have Been a Missed Opportunity

A few days ago, I received an inquiry from a sourcing agent. The client was in a hurry – I could tell from the fact that they had sent the same inquiry to both email addresses listed on our website, and when they didn't hear back quickly enough, they asked their staff to call me directly.

I picked up the phone. On the other end, Gabbi made it clear: they needed a decision fast.

"I'm actually in the middle of discussing your housing finish with our shell supplier right now," I told her. "We're evaluating whether the gradient effect you're looking for is achievable. I'll have an answer in two hours."

She sounded relieved. "I'll wait for your email."

## The Design Brief: A Custom Gradient Housing

Two hours later, our art designer had prepared two gradient concept renderings based on their initial request:

**Option A:** 1/2 Blue + 1/2 Pink (balanced split)
**Option B:** 1/4 Pink + 3/4 Blue (subtle accent)

We also pointed out that the metallic plating on the device body could be adjusted to match – a small detail that makes a significant difference in the final visual impact.

The client chose Option B. It matched the reference sample they had sent us perfectly.

## The Question That Made Me Rethink How We Quote

We sent over the price. It was higher than the market average.

Gabbi's response was sharp and fair: *"Your pricing is above what we typically see. Please show us where the added value is – I need to see it reflected in the quote."*

That was the moment I realized I had been making a mistake.

I had been quoting prices without clearly explaining what the client was actually paying for. I assumed the value was self-evident – it wasn't. If you don't articulate why your product costs more, the client will always assume you're just charging more.

So I laid out the facts clearly.

## Where the Extra Cost Goes – A Transparent Breakdown

Here's what I shared with Gabbi:

| Component | Our Source | Market Alternative |
|-----------|------------|-------------------|
| **Capacitor** | Same vendor as Silk'n | Unknown/Unbranded |
| **Optical Reflector** | Same vendor as Sensica | Unknown/Unbranded |
| **IPL Lamp (Xenon Flash)** | Excelitas – industry standard | Unnamed suppliers |

Most manufacturers in this space don't use high-grade components across all three critical parts. They cut corners on one or two to keep the BOM low. The result? A device that works fine when new – but starts losing energy output after 6 to 12 months of regular use.

Why? Because the internal components degrade. The capacitor loses its ability to store charge consistently. The reflector tarnishes and scatters light. The lamp tube degrades with every flash.

When that happens, the user feels it: weaker pulses, longer treatment times, less effective hair reduction. The product turns into something it was never meant to be – disappointing.

We don't build products that degrade after a year. We build products that maintain stable energy output over time – because we use the same caliber of components that brands like Silk'n and Sensica rely on.

**That's where the extra cost goes. Not into packaging, not into marketing fluff – into the parts that actually matter.**

## Two Vendors, One Sample Run – Why We Double-Track Everything

Gabbi approved the pricing. But we didn't stop there.

We contacted two different housing manufacturers simultaneously and commissioned both to produce gradient shell samples. We will choose the one that delivers the better gradient finish – and that becomes our production partner for this order.

Why do we do it this way?

1. **Better results** – Competition between two vendors pushes both to perform at their best.
2. **Redundancy** – If one supplier hits a production bottleneck or quality issue, the other is already qualified and ready to step in. We never put ourselves – or our clients – in a position where we're dependent on a single source with no fallback.

This isn't just "having backup." It's how we operate. When you work with iShine, you're not just getting a factory that can produce your product – you're getting a factory that's actively managing its supply chain to ensure quality and reliability at every level.

## What We Learned – and What We'll Do Differently

This project reinforced something important.

A quote isn't just a number. It's a statement of value. If the number is higher than the market average, the client needs to understand why – not in abstract terms, but in concrete, comparable, verifiable details.

So moving forward, we are making a change: every quote will include a transparent breakdown of the key components we use, the brands we source from, and why that matters for long-term performance.

No more assuming the client will "just know" why we cost more.

## Our Takeaway

The client got their gradient housing – exactly the finish they wanted. We got a reminder that the value of what we build is not obvious unless we take the time to tell the story.

And that's okay. Because when the story is built on facts – better components, better vendor choices, better redundancy planning – it's a story worth telling.

**You bring the brief. We'll bring the options, the vendors, and the execution. And we'll tell you exactly why it costs what it costs.**
