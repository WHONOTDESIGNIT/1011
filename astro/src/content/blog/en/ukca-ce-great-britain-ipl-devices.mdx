**Short answer:** since 2024 Great Britain recognises CE marking indefinitely for around twenty-one product regulations, electrical equipment and EMC among them. A valid CE mark is enough to sell there. UKCA is optional, not withdrawn, and Northern Ireland is a different market that still runs on EU rules.

## The mistake runs in both directions

One buyer asks for a UKCA certificate before the first order, because their retailer's onboarding form has a field for it. Another tells me the UK is "the same as the EU now" and ships without checking the plug.

Both are partly right, which is why this market stays confusing. CE is accepted. The product still has to arrive in a form a British customer can plug in. Those are separate questions.

## What the indefinite recognition covers

The policy is broad, not universal. It covers most consumer hardware regulations, in the same family that UKCA was designed to mirror:

| Regulation area | Position in Great Britain |
|---|---|
| Electrical safety | CE accepted |
| Electromagnetic compatibility | CE accepted |
| Radio equipment | CE accepted |
| Hazardous substances | CE accepted; UK RoHS is a separate instrument running alongside |
| Medical devices | Not covered by the general policy; MHRA runs its own timeline |
| Construction products | Not covered; separate regime |

For an IPL device placed as consumer electrical equipment, that lands you in the first four rows. A CE technical file, a CE declaration of conformity and the CE mark are the basis for the GB market.

## The 2027 detail, and why it rarely bites

Two transitional rules remain for anyone who chooses to use UKCA anyway:

- The mark can appear on a label or an accompanying document rather than directly on the product, until 31 December 2027.
- Conformity assessment carried out under CE rules and completed by the end of 2024 can be used as the basis for a UKCA declaration, until the same date.

Neither matters if you are simply relying on CE. They matter if a British customer insists on UKCA, which happens mainly when their own retailer's paperwork was written before the policy change and nobody has updated it.

The useful response to that request is a question: which regulation are you asking about, and is there a document that says CE is not accepted for it? In most cases the answer is that the form is older than the policy.

## The medical device exception, which is the live one

If you take the medical route rather than the consumer electrical one, the UK stops being simple. The MHRA has its own timeline, and it has been moving.

A consultation running from February to April 2026 proposed extending the current transitional window to the end of 2028, and separately proposed recognising CE-marked devices under the EU regulations indefinitely rather than to a fixed date. Until the outcome is confirmed, treat any 2028 to 2030 date you see quoted as provisional.

That is the one place in this market where I would not rely on a blog post, including this one. Ask the UK body handling the file for the current position in writing.

## Northern Ireland is not Great Britain

The recognition policy covers England, Scotland and Wales. Northern Ireland applies EU product rules directly, so an IPL device sold there needs CE marking and EU-facing paperwork on EU terms, no matter what GB policy says.

In practice most manufacturers ship the same CE-marked product to both, which works. What does not work is a label and manual prepared for GB only, without the EU responsible person details, then sent to Belfast.

## What genuinely differs, and belongs in the label block

| Item | Great Britain | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Mark | CE accepted | UKCA optional |
| Plug | BS 1363 | A EU plug in the box is a returns problem, not a legal one, until somebody complains |
| Mains | 230 V, 50 Hz | Within the range most adapters already cover, but confirm the adapter rating |
| Importer details | On the product or packaging | The GB importer, not the factory |
| Manual language | English | No second language requirement in GB |
| Hazardous substances | UK RoHS | A separate instrument from the EU restriction list |

The importer line is the one that gets skipped, and it is the same structural point as every other market in this series: the party answerable inside the territory has to be identifiable on the product. In GB that is the importer. The "UK responsible person" mandate that people ask about belongs to the medical device regime and to certain other sectors; it is not a general requirement for electrical goods, and adding one to your label does not make you more compliant.
