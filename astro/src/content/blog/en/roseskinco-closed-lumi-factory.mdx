RoseSkinCo has closed its online store. Seven years, and then a page that reads:

> "RoseSkinCo has officially closed its online store and is no longer accepting new orders. We're deeply grateful to every customer who supported us and made RoseSkinCo part of their journey over the years."

That notice is still up at [roseskinco.com](https://roseskinco.com), next to a line about 400,000+ customers. We make the devices they sold. So this is written from the factory side, because that's the only side we can speak to honestly.

## Seven years, and the timing that made them

RoseSkinCo came to us in 2019. At-home IPL was not yet a category anyone took seriously, and they were early enough that being early looked like a mistake.

Then 2020 happened. Salons closed. People who had never considered a device at home started buying one. The OG tag RoseSkinCo videos crossed a million views on YouTube, then two million, then three. Nobody in this industry had seen a single product carry a brand that far.

What made the difference wasn't the hardware. Hardware gets copied, and it did get copied. Website layouts, ad copy, marketing assets, all of it showed up on competitor sites within months. The thing that couldn't be copied was the co-founder's taste. A photographer from Argentina shooting hot and unbothered when everyone else was shooting soft and clinical. Pink used like a dare. A hundred dollars in store credit for posting a photo, back when nobody offered that.

Taste is the part of a brand you can't buy from a contract manufacturer. We can tool a housing and match a Pantone. We can't decide that the category is boring and needs to be set on fire.

## Three devices, and what each one had to prove

**OG** was the proof that a no-name factory could build something a brand could charge real money for. It worked because RoseSkinCo kept pushing on the parts that were expensive to change.

**Lumi** was where the platform became a platform. Same light source family, better thermal design, a housing that looked deliberate instead of assembled.

**Lumi 2** was the hard one. The brief kept moving toward thinner. Thinner housing means less room for heat to go, and heat is the entire engineering problem in an at-home IPL device. We went through housing revisions and cooling layouts that we would not have attempted for a client who accepted the first sample. The result is the thinnest device we have put into volume production. You can argue about whether thinness matters to a buyer. It mattered to them, and that is what got built. Read the [full development timeline](/blog/ipl-hair-removal-device-development-timeline) if you want the engineering sequence rather than the marketing version.

## What the FDA record actually shows about who built Lumi

This is the part some of you already found on your own, and we would rather explain it than leave it to guesswork.

Search the FDA's 510(k) database for K222537 and the entry reads:

- **Device:** IPL Hair Removal Device
- **Models:** skn001, skn005, skn006, skn002, RoseSkinCo Lumi
- **Applicant:** Shenzhen Ishine Technology Company Limited
- **Decision:** Cleared, October 14, 2022
- **Product code:** OHT (Light Based Over-the-counter Hair Removal)
- **Class:** II, special controls
- **Regulation:** [21 CFR 878.4810](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-H/part-878/subpart-E/section-878.4810)

Read the model list again. It has both the brand name and the factory's internal model numbers in the same field. That is standard practice for a 510(k) held by a contract manufacturer: the clearance belongs to the entity that submitted it, and every device it covers gets listed by name.

Here's the part worth understanding. **The clearance was never RoseSkinCo's asset.** It sits with Shenzhen Ishine Technology. That is the structural difference between owning a brand and owning a manufacturing capability, and it is why a store closing and a product line ending are two different events.

You can run this lookup on any device, including ones you already own:

1. Find the model number on the device label or the charging base.
2. Search the [FDA 510(k) premarket notification database](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfPMN/pmn.cfm) by applicant name or by product code.
3. Check the [product code OHT classification entry](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfPCD/classification.cfm?ID=OHT) to see what the code actually covers.
4. Compare the decision date against the device you're holding. A number from 2019 is not a clearance in 2026, and the database settles it either way.

Most buyers never do this. The sellers who count on that are the reason it's worth five minutes. We wrote a longer piece on [how regulators classify home IPL devices](/blog/how-regulators-classify-home-ipl) if you want the classification rules rather than one record.

## Can you still buy Lumi and Lumi 2?

Yes. The devices are available and shipping. A closed storefront on one website doesn't stop a production line, and it doesn't retire a platform that's still being manufactured.

If you want to see what the current generation looks like, start with the [Lumi 2 platform](/products/lumi-2), or the [original Lumi](/products/lumi) for the earlier configuration. If you're a brand owner who watched this happen and wants to understand what a manufacturing partnership actually commits to in writing, the [OEM vs ODM breakdown](/blog/02-oem-vs-odm-choosing-the-right-manufacturing-model) covers that side.

## What changes for people who already own a RoseSkinCo device

Nothing about the hardware. Your device runs on the same firmware and the same light source it always did, and no server going dark changes that. At-home IPL devices don't need a subscription to emit light.

For support, RoseSkinCo's own notice says their customer support team remains available. For parts, filters, and technical questions about the Lumi platform specifically, you can reach us directly through [our contact page](/contact) and we'll tell you plainly what we can and can't do for a device bought through another company.

You should hear the honest version of that: we were the manufacturer, and the warranty you were sold was RoseSkinCo's to honour. We'll help where we can, and we won't pretend a manufacturer's goodwill is the same thing as a contract you hold.

## The question we keep getting

**Why did they close?**

The team is taking an extended break. That's their statement and their call to make, and we're not going to dress it up with reasons they didn't publish.

What we can tell you is what our side of it is: the partnership is paused, not cancelled. Seven years of working with people who push harder than the market requires buys a lot of patience from us. If they come back, we'll build for them again.

## What the last seven years taught us on the factory floor

A brand that shows up with real taste will outperform a brand with a bigger ad budget. We have watched that play out across [a lot of client work](/clients), and the pattern holds every time.

Second: the copyable parts of a business get copied fast, and that is fine. What protects you is the part nobody can reproduce, which is usually the taste of one or two people.

Third, and this one took a while to sink in: brand owners need to know which assets are theirs. You own the name, the audience, and the look. You don't own the clearance, the tooling, or the model numbers. Knowing which is which before you sign is worth more than any price negotiation. [Building a brand from zero](/blog/zero-to-one-beauty-brand) is mostly a lesson in that distinction.

## Thank you

To the RoseSkinCo team: you took a category that was being sold on spec sheets and made people want the object. OG, Lumi, Lumi 2. That's three platforms a lot of brands would trade their whole catalogue for.

Seven years is a real run. The friendship outlasts the storefront, and that part isn't going anywhere.

If you're reading this because you were a RoseSkinCo customer, thank you for buying the thing we built. You're the reason any of it existed.
