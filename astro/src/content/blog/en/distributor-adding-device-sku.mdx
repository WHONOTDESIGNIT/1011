A powered IPL device is the first product in an accessories portfolio that carries a plug, a battery, a warranty and a certificate. Four parts of the business change when it joins the range: certification, warranty, returns and the sales conversation. Pricing the unit works the same way it always has.

## What an accessory range already covers

Landing a range in a retailer and running a warehouse work the same way for a device. Pricing works the same way until the warranty reserve enters the calculation, and that is where the accessory experience stops helping. A powered device adds a document set for each destination market, a two-year liability on every unit sold, and a counter conversation about skin that nobody in the accessory business has had before.

## The four changes

| Change | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| Certification | One device, a separate document set per market. A file that clears one country may stop at the next border. |
| Warranty | A two-year commitment on every unit already sold. The reserve belongs on the balance sheet from the first shipment. |
| Returns and spares | A written process before the first return arrives. Without one, the first return sets the process. |
| Sales support | Retailers ask questions about skin, medication and results. Somebody has to answer them accurately. |

## The warranty reserve

Two years of cover on a device means a share of this year's sales returns as claims next year. A SKU priced without that reserve reports a margin in month one and a loss in month fourteen.

Ask the manufacturer for the field failure rate of the platform on offer. A supplier with no such number on file has not tracked field failures.

## Returns and spare parts

Devices come back for reasons that need different routes: a unit that stopped working, a customer who could not work out how to use it, a customer who changed her mind. The list lengthens in each market a distributor enters, and every kind needs its route written before the stock lands.

Spare parts raise a second question: how long the supplier holds parts for a model it has superseded. The answer decides how much stock a distributor can hold without stranding it.

## The sales conversation

An accessory sells on price and on sight. A device sells after an explanation: which areas it suits, how often to use it, who should not use it at all. A retailer buying the SKU will ask who provides that explanation.

A distributor who answers "the manual" leaves the question to the shop floor. Retail staff hear it several times a week and need an answer they can repeat.

## What to ask before signing

- Which markets the device is already certified for, and which need further work.
- Who holds the warranty: the distributor, or the factory behind it.
- How long spare parts remain available after the model is superseded.
- What the minimum order is per model, and whether mixed models count toward it.
- What documentation ships in the carton, in which languages.

The language question decides more sales than the certificate does. A device certified for a market with a manual in the wrong language sits on the shelf, and certification does not move it.

## What the device does to the basket

Accessories move on impulse. A device moves on a question, and the question brings the customer back to the counter. Distributors we supply tell us that one supported SKU lifts accessory revenue in the same accounts over the next two quarters, because the buyer has a reason to discuss the category. That is an impression from conversations. No distributor has given us numbers for it yet.

The effect is measurable in a distributor own accounts, before the second order. It is also the argument a board can check.

## What the board will ask

Basket effect: accessory revenue per account in the quarter after the device landed, set against the quarter before. This number decides whether the SKU becomes a category.

Warranty reserve as a percentage of device revenue. Two years of cover on sold units sits on the balance sheet, and a proposal that omits it misstates the margin.

Service load: minutes of support per unit sold, multiplied by the units planned. The result shows whether the SKU needs a hire, and a hire changes the case.

## The first order as a test

One model, one market, one retailer. Mixed models in a first order reduce the per-unit freight and teach nothing, because results from two models cannot be compared.

Ask for the certification file for that market, the warranty wording in writing and the spare parts policy before the deposit. Keep the order small enough that a failed listing costs a quarter of margin. A year of margin is the number this order exists to avoid losing.

## Terms this supplier publishes

Devices ship with the test reports and declarations for the destination markets, the warranty term appears in writing, and mixed-model first orders are accepted. Warehouse operation, warranty reserve and exclusivity stay with the distributor unless a target is agreed.

## Where the detail comes from

- [Regulation (EU) 2023/988](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/988/oj/eng) — the general product safety rules that cover the consumer device.
- [IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations](https://www.iata.org/en/programs/cargo/dgr/) — the air-freight rules that classify a device with a lithium cell.
- [CIE S 009 / IEC 62471](https://cie.co.at/publications/photobiological-safety-lamps-and-lamp-systems) — the photobiological safety standard every IPL lamp is measured against.
