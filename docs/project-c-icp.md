# Project C0 — Ideal Customer Profile and demand hypotheses

**Status: hypotheses only. No market size figures are invented here; every claim below is
marked with how it could be disproved.**

The question this document answers is narrow: **who should iShine be talking to, and what
would prove us wrong about them?**

## What is already established (from B0 / B1, not assumed)

- Natural search is a consumer-education asset: 28,100 impressions in 90 days, commercial
  intent roughly 2.5% of them.
- `ipl oem`, `ipl odm`, `custom ipl hair removal device`, `ipl handset manufacturer` produced
  **zero impressions in 90 days**. There is no inbound B2B search demand to scale yet.
- The home page is the only page that both ranks (9.95) and clicks (42 in 90 days).

Conclusion carried forward: **inbound is not the constraint right now. Knowing who to
approach is.**

## Product and service facts that define the ICP

These constrain which customers iShine can actually serve, so they come before the segments:

| fact | consequence for ICP |
|---|---|
| Home-use IPL devices, 14 platforms (Lumi, Lumi 2, Venus, Hestia, Hebe, Themis, Euno, Alpha, Emerald, Helix, Eirene, Wooden, LED mask 001A/002A, LED panel F618) | consumer-brand customers, not clinics or medspas buying professional machines |
| MOQ from 500 units | rules out pre-revenue founders and one-off buyers; suits brands with an existing retail channel |
| OEM/ODM: concept, engineering, tooling, certification, production | suits customers who need a full program, not a rebadger of stock |
| Certifications supported: FDA 510(k), CE/MDR, IEC 62471, EMC, UN38.3; regional PSE, KC, TGA, SASO, GPSR | suits customers selling into regulated markets who cannot use a non-compliant factory |
| 24-month warranty, 0.5–1% free spares per shipment, 15-day return window | suits customers with after-sales obligations, i.e. real brands |
| Dropshipping support, custom packaging, custom manuals, logo printing | suits DTC-first brands without warehousing |
| DDP and standard air freight priced per market | suits buyers who need landed cost before committing |

## Four ICP segments

Each segment states the need, the fit, the decision maker, the signal that would confirm it,
and **what would show the hypothesis is wrong**.

### S1 — Established IPL brand seeking a second supplier

- **Need**: supply security and better margin on an existing, already-selling IPL line.
- **iShine fit**: high. Drop-in platforms, same certification set, MOQ 500 suits a re-order.
- **Decision maker**: head of sourcing / supply chain, not marketing.
- **Confirming signal**: they ask about lead time, capacity, and switching cost before they ask
  about price.
- **Wrong if**: they only want a cheaper unit at the same quality — that is a price
  negotiation, and iShine's certification depth is wasted.
- **Why this one first**: the shortest conversation to a PO, and the least education needed.

### S2 — Beauty brand without an IPL device, launching one

- **Need**: a full OEM/ODM program from concept to certified product.
- **iShine fit**: high on capability, **but** the sales cycle is the longest and the failure
  rate the highest.
- **Decision maker**: founder or product director.
- **Confirming signal**: they can describe their target market, price point and channel — not
  just "we want an IPL".
- **Wrong if**: they have no distribution yet. MOQ 500 plus certification cost is not
  survivable for a brand with no channel; those conversations consume time and close never.

### S3 — Distributor or专业 channel buying devices to resell

- **Need**: reliable stock, predictable landed cost, documentation for their market.
- **iShine fit**: medium-high. DDP pricing per market and regional certification are the
  actual product here.
- **Decision maker**: purchasing manager.
- **Confirming signal**: they name specific markets and ask for landed cost or certification
  documents.
- **Wrong if**: they want to resell without registration obligations — in regulated markets
  that becomes iShine's liability.

### S4 — Company needing OEM/ODM capability, not necessarily an IPL

- **Need**: a manufacturing partner for a beauty device program.
- **iShine fit**: medium. Capability transfers; the sales narrative does not.
- **Decision maker**: product or engineering lead.
- **Confirming signal**: an RFQ with a spec, a target price and a timeline.
- **Wrong if**: they are exploring, not buying. Treat as pipeline, not forecast.

## Ranked priority

| rank | segment | why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **S1** | shortest cycle, existing budget, certifications are the differentiator |
| 2 | **S3** | clear purchasing process, repeated orders if the first shipment lands |
| 3 | **S2** | highest value per deal, longest cycle, needs qualification discipline |
| 4 | **S4** | opportunistic; respond, do not prospect |

Not targeted: consumers, clinics and medspas buying professional machines, dropship
hobbyists, and anyone below MOQ 500.

## How this stage is measured

Not page count, not impressions, not emails sent.

| metric | what it proves |
|---|---|
| qualified target accounts identified | the market exists on paper |
| valid contacts reached | decision makers are reachable |
| positive replies | partner interest is real |
| substantive commercial conversations | a real requirement exists |
| RFQs received | buying behaviour |

A site visit is not a lead. An email sent is not outreach. A reply is not an inquiry.

## Falsification plan

This document is a set of guesses. It should be revised when:

1. S1 outreach produces no positive replies after a defined number of attempts — then the
   "supply security" need is not what the market feels.
2. S2 conversations never reach a spec or a target price — then the segment is a fantasy
   pipeline.
3. Replies cluster in a segment not listed here — then this ICP is wrong and should be
   rewritten around the data, not defended.
